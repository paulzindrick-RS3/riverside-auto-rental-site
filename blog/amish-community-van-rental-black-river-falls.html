Driving for Amish Families? Rent Our 15-Passenger Van

Weddings, funerals, auctions and family visits. One van gets the whole group there in one trip.

Around Black River Falls, plenty of neighbors pick up extra work driving Amish families where a buggy can't go: a wedding two counties over, a funeral in another settlement, a doctor's appointment in La Crosse, or a visit to family across the state. Lately we've had more of those drivers calling about our 15-passenger van. Here's what you need to know before you book.

Why a 15-passenger van

Amish families tend to travel together, and a wedding or funeral often means a dozen people heading the same way. In a car or minivan that's two or three trips. The van gets everyone there in one, with room for coats, food and bags.

What it costs

  • 15-passenger van: $135/day plus $0.10/mile, or $750/week for Jackson County renters.
  • 7-passenger minivan for smaller groups: $80/day plus $0.10/mile, or $450/week for local renters.

All rates are plus sales tax. Out-of-town weekly van rates are quoted by phone. A 120-mile round trip to a wedding in the van comes to $135 plus $12 in mileage: about $147 before tax. The 15-passenger cost guide has more examples.

What you need to rent

  • Be 18 or older, with a valid U.S. driver's license
  • Proof of full-coverage auto insurance that covers you, showing the policy number and expiration date
  • A card for the deposit: $300 for Jackson County renters, $500 for out-of-town (Visa, Mastercard, Discover, or a bank debit card linked to a checking account)

The driver is the renter. The families riding along don't need to sign anything, but anyone who drives has to be listed on the rental.

Paid to drive? Check your insurance first

Our renters use their own auto insurance, and Riverside doesn't sell any. Many personal auto policies don't cover carrying passengers for pay, and some exclude large passenger vans. If you're being paid to drive, call your agent before you book and ask two questions:

  1. Does my policy cover a rented 15-passenger van?
  2. Does it cover me when I'm paid to drive passengers?

If the answer to either is no, sort that out before the trip. It protects you and the families riding with you. Our insurance guide has more questions worth asking.

Trips worth planning ahead

  • Weddings: the busy stretch runs late fall into winter, after harvest. Book early.
  • Funerals: these come up on short notice. Call and we'll do what we can.
  • Medical appointments: La Crosse, Eau Claire, Marshfield and Madison runs.
  • Auctions and family visits: a day trip or a full week on the road.

Driving out of state is fine within the lower 48, as long as the van comes back to us in Black River Falls. In winter, check 511 Wisconsin before you leave and give a full van extra room to stop.
